Yes. You don't need a major label, and you don't need to be an RIAA member. The RIAA's Gold & Platinum Program is open to members and non-members, and it accepts sales data from your distributor or from detailed artist royalty statements.

Plenty of independent artists qualify and never apply, because nobody applies for them. Certification isn't automatic. Someone has to request it and pay for the audit.

## What it costs

From the RIAA's application page:

- **Non-members:** $450 per certification level, paid up front, no bulk discounts
- **RIAA members:** $350 per title, with bulk discounts for several titles on one request

The fee is non-refundable. If your request doesn't meet the program rules, you're still charged. Check your numbers before you apply.

If a single has already passed Platinum, a non-member applying for Gold and Platinum at the same time pays per level.

## What you'll need

The audit is run by Gelfand, Rennert & Feldman (GR&F) on the RIAA's behalf. They'll ask for:

1. **A US sales summary by store.** On-demand streams and paid downloads only, US only, broken out by ISRC, store and format. No user-generated content.
2. **Label copy.** A one-sheet for every ISRC (singles) or UPC (albums): artist, title, version, runtime and credits.
3. **Supporting statements** if the auditor asks. Distributor statements or artist royalty statements work.

You have 30 days from the date you apply to hand everything over, or the request can be denied.

## Who should apply

Whoever holds the sales data, usually the label. If you self-release through a distributor like DistroKid, TuneCore or CD Baby, you are the label. You can apply yourself.

If you're signed, ask your label. They may be able to bundle it with other titles at the member rate.

## The step most people skip

Before paying $450, know where you stand. Distributor dashboards show worldwide totals and mix in plays that don't count, so they make you look closer than you are.
