An album unit is one album sale. The RIAA lets you count it three ways, and you can mix all three:

- **1 album download** (or physical album) = 1 unit
- **10 track downloads** from the album = 1 unit
- **1,500 on-demand US streams** of the album's tracks = 1 unit

Gold is 500,000 units, Platinum is 1,000,000, and Diamond is 10,000,000.

## Every track pulls weight

The streams of every song on the album count toward the album. That includes the singles you released before the album came out, as long as they're on some version of it: standard, deluxe, clean or explicit.

So a 12-track album where the lead single did 400 million US streams already has about 267,000 units from that one song.

## Singles can count twice

A song can earn its own single certification and still count toward the album. The single and album programs are separate. A hit single and its album often go Gold around the same time.

## What doesn't carry over

- **Tracks not on any version of the album.** A remix that only came out as a standalone single doesn't count.
- **Bonus tracks exclusive to one store** only count from that store, and the label has to confirm it in writing.
- **Songs you're featured on.** A collaboration counts toward your album only if you're a primary artist on it.
- **Tracks already used for a Greatest Hits certification.** No track gets counted twice.

## EPs

EPs certify on the same 500,000-unit scale. The RIAA's Shortform Album/EP category requires three to five different songs and a maximum run time of 30 minutes.

## Quick math

| | Album downloads | Track downloads | US streams | Units |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example | 20,000 | 150,000 | 600,000,000 | 20,000 + 15,000 + 400,000 = **435,000** |

That album needs 65,000 more units for Gold, or about 97.5 million more US streams.
